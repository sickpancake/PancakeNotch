How can we help?

Quick answers for the things people ask most, in plain steps. PancakeNotch has a Help page of its own too: open the PancakeNotch window and pick Help in the sidebar. It checks how things are on your Mac right now. To see the first-run welcome again, open About and click Show Welcome Again.

macOS says it can't verify the developer #

That's expected the first time. PancakeNotch isn't notarized by Apple, because that needs a paid developer account, so macOS asks you to confirm that you trust it.

  1. Make sure PancakeNotch is in your Applications folder, then open it.
  2. When macOS says it can't verify the developer, click Done. Don't choose Move to Trash.
  3. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security.
  4. Scroll down to the message about PancakeNotch and click Open Anyway.
  5. Confirm with Open Anyway, and enter your password if macOS asks.

You only need to do this once. Updates install without these steps.

Open it from Applications, not from your Downloads folder or the zip file. That way updates can replace it in place.

The notch doesn't show or open #

When nothing is playing, the closed notch is plain black, exactly like the hardware, so it looks just as it did before. Rest the pointer on it and it opens. If it doesn't, go through these:

  • Is the notch switched on?Open the PancakeNotch window: the switch on Home says whether the notch is on. You can also click the PancakeNotch icon in the menu bar and choose Turn Notch On.
  • Is the pointer on the MacBook's own screen?The notch lives on the built-in screen. With an external display, move the pointer up to the top of the MacBook's screen. With the lid closed, there's no notch to use.
  • Is something playing full screen?PancakeNotch can hide the notch while a full-screen video plays. Move the pointer and it comes back, or change the setting (see Full screen).
  • Is PancakeNotch running?Open it from your Applications folder. If you've hidden the menu bar icon, opening the app again shows its window.

Now Playing doesn't show my music #

Album art and sound bars appear beside the notch while something plays, and the controls show when you open it.

  • Play something on this MacMusic, Spotify, or a podcast or video in your browser. Give it a second to show up.
  • Check that Now Playing is onIn the PancakeNotch window, open Modules and make sure Now Playing is switched on.
  • Check the connectionUnder Modules › Now Playing settings, Connection says whether PancakeNotch can see what's playing. If something's wrong, click Try Again. If that doesn't help, quit PancakeNotch and open it again.
  • Limited: only Music and SpotifySometimes macOS doesn't share what other apps are playing. PancakeNotch then still shows Music and Spotify (Spotify without album art). Try Again often brings everything back.
  • Is the app ignored?Apps on the Ignored apps list in Now Playing settings never show. Click Show Again to bring one back.
  • Nothing beside the notch, but the song shows when it's open?Turn on Show album art and sound bars beside the notch in Now Playing settings.

"Allow Control…": controlling Music and Spotify #

In limited mode, PancakeNotch talks to Music and Spotify directly. macOS asks your permission for that the first time it's needed, and only then. Click Allow.

If you said no, the open notch shows Allow control, and Now Playing settings show Allow Control…. Without it, the buttons can't control that app, and shuffle, repeat and Resume stay hidden for it. To change your answer:

  1. Click Allow Control… (or Allow control in the notch). It opens the right place in System Settings.
  2. Or open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Automation yourself.
  3. Find PancakeNotch and switch on Music and/or Spotify.

Sound bars and "Follow the music" #

The little bars beside the notch can move in three ways. Choose under Modules › Now Playing settings › Sound bars:

  • Gentle motion (the default): the bars dance while the playing app makes sound and rest when it's quiet. No permission needed.
  • Follow the music: the bars move with the actual sound of the app that's playing.
  • Still: the bars stand still.

Follow the music needs macOS's System Audio Recording permission. macOS asks the first time the bars listen, and shows a purple dot in the menu bar while they do. PancakeNotch listens only to the app that's playing, only while the bars are showing, and never records or saves any sound.

If you said no, the bars use Gentle motion instead. To change your mind, click Open Privacy Settings in Now Playing settings, switch on PancakeNotch under System Audio Recording, then click Try Again.

With Reduce Motion turned on in macOS, the bars stay still.

The notch hides in full screen #

So it doesn't get in the way of a film, PancakeNotch can hide the notch while an app fills the screen. Choose under Notch › Hide the notch in full screen:

  • While watching (the default): hidden while a full-screen video plays and you're not moving the pointer. Move it and the notch is back.
  • Always: hidden whenever an app fills the screen, like a video or a game.
  • Never: works as usual in full-screen apps.

While it's hidden, your keyboard shortcut still opens it. If you don't have one yet, set it on the Keyboard page (see Keyboard shortcut).

For games and other apps where you never want it, use Always hide for these apps just below: click Add App… and choose the app. While a listed app fills the screen, the notch stays fully hidden, even from your shortcut. Click Remove next to an app to take it off the list.

Setting a keyboard shortcut #

A shortcut opens and closes the notch from any app, even while it's hidden in full screen.

  1. Open the PancakeNotch window and choose Keyboard (or click Set a Shortcut on Home).
  2. Click the box next to Open and close the notch.
  3. Press the keys you want, for example ⌥⌘N. Esc cancels and Delete clears the shortcut.
  • It must include ⌘ Command, ⌥ Option or ⌃ Control (a function key on its own works too).
  • Shortcuts macOS and every app rely on, like ⌘Q or ⌘Space, can't be used.
  • If PancakeNotch says another app already uses it, or the shortcut does nothing, choose a different one.

How the Shelf works #

Drag files, images, text or links onto the notch. It opens and keeps them on the Shelf until you need them, even after a restart.

What it keeps

  • Files stay where they are. The Shelf doesn't copy them: it only remembers where to find them. Move or rename a file and the Shelf follows it. Delete it and it leaves the Shelf the next time the notch opens.
  • Things that aren't files yet, like a picture dragged from a web page, a photo from an app that hands it over only when dropped, or a long piece of text, are saved in the Shelf's own folder and deleted when you remove them.
  • Short notes and links are kept as they are. Click one to edit it.

Using it

  • Click an item for a big preview, or to edit a note or link. Right-click for Copy, Quick Look, Show in Finder, AirDrop and more.
  • Drag an item out to use it. Dragging a file out copies it by default; under Modules › Shelf settings you can choose Move instead, or remove items once you've dragged them out.
  • While you drag something onto the notch, an AirDrop area appears: drop there to send it straight away without keeping it.

Removing items

  • Click an item, then the trash button. Or select it and press Delete, or right-click and choose Remove.
  • Clear all removes everything; click it a second time to confirm.
  • Removing an item never deletes your original file.
  • To tidy up by itself, set Clear the Shelf after to 1 day, 1 week or 1 month in Shelf settings.

When it's full

The Shelf holds 20 items. From 15, a small tray with the count shows beside the notch. When it's full, the notch tells you what didn't fit: click Clear all, or Choose items to remove to make room, and the waiting items are added.

When a drop doesn't land

  • Mission Control opens instead. macOS opens Mission Control when you drag something to the very top of the screen. The notch opens before that, so drop a little lower, on the open notch. Or turn off Drag windows to top of screen to enter Mission Control in System Settings › Desktop & Dock.
  • Use the MacBook's own screen. The notch is only on the built-in display.
  • The Shelf is switched off. Turn it on under Modules.

When an item disappears

The Shelf only remembers where your files are. Moved or renamed files stay on it; a deleted file leaves the Shelf the next time the notch opens. If Clear the Shelf after is set in Shelf settings, older items are removed by themselves.

Agents: see your AI agents #

If you use AI coding agents, the Agents module lists every thread that's running on your Mac, from Claude Code, Codex and Cursor (Cursor once you've connected it, see Connect). It works with no setup: PancakeNotch reads the files those tools already keep on your Mac.

Turning it on

  1. Open the PancakeNotch window and choose Modules.
  2. Switch on Agents. It's offered once PancakeNotch finds Claude Code or Codex on your Mac.
  3. Open the notch and choose the Agents tab, or pick Agents in the Module Menu.

What a row shows

Each thread has its own row: the tool's icon, the thread's name, its project folder, its status with the time, and what it's doing right now, like "Editing NotchView.swift" or "Running tests". Click a row to bring that tool's app forward: Terminal, Ghostty, VS Code, T3 Code, the Codex app and so on.

Agents settings

In Modules, open Agents' Settings to choose:

  • Name agents by: the conversation's Title as Claude Code or Codex keeps it (its project folder until it has one), the Last message you sent it, or the git Branch it works on.
  • Show agents from: leave out Claude Code, Codex or Cursor.
  • Keep finished agents: 5 minutes, 30 minutes, or until you click Clear finished.
  • Whether to show what each agent is doing, and its subagents.

What the statuses mean

  • Working (a dotted circle): the agent is busy.
  • Needs you (a white circle with a !): the agent is waiting for you, either to allow something or to answer a question.
  • Done (a check in a circle): the thread has finished. It leaves the list 30 minutes after you've seen it (you can change this in Settings).
  • Stopped (a square in a circle): the thread was stopped, or its tool quit.
  • Error (an x in a circle): something went wrong in that thread.

Each mark has its own color so you can tell them apart at a glance: blue for Working, orange for Needs you, green for Done, gray for Stopped and red for Error.

Subagents

When an agent hands part of its work to helpers (Claude Code's subagents, or agents that Codex spawns), they don't get rows of their own. The thread's row shows a small 2 subagents button instead: click it to see each helper's task and how long it has been working, and click again to hide them. Helpers leave the list as soon as they finish. Helper threads that another app starts as separate conversations, like T3 Code's, still show as threads of their own, because nothing in the tools' files says which thread they belong to.

With the notch closed

One side of the closed notch shows a small mark and a number: how many threads are working. It changes to a ! when one needs you, to an x when one stopped with an error, and to a ✓ when one has finished. A slim strip also pops out under the notch for a moment when a thread needs you, or when one finishes after working for 30 seconds or more. It never pops out for an app you're already looking at.

Connect: instant alerts and Allow/Deny #

Agents works without any setup. If you Connect a tool, it tells PancakeNotch what it's doing straight away, so alerts are instant. For Claude Code and Codex, you also get Allow and Deny buttons right in the notch. Connecting is optional, and you choose it for each tool on its own.

Connecting a tool

  1. Open the PancakeNotch window and choose Modules › Agents settings.
  2. Click Connect next to the tool.
  3. Codex only: Codex asks you to trust new hooks once. Open Codex and type /hooks, then trust the PancakeNotch one.

What Connect changes

It adds a small PancakeNotch helper to the tool's hook settings. Hooks are the tool's own way of telling other programs what it's doing. The helper goes in one file for each tool:

  • Claude Code: ~/.claude/settings.json
  • Codex: ~/.codex/hooks.json
  • Cursor: ~/.cursor/hooks.json

Disconnect removes only that helper and leaves everything else in the file as it was. Cursor gets status only: no Allow or Deny.

Allow, Deny and Open

When a Claude Code or Codex agent asks permission to do something, its row shows three buttons:

  • Allow lets it do that one thing, once.
  • Deny tells it no.
  • Open switches to the tool's app and hands the question back there, so you answer it in the tool itself.

Codex requests that can be handled automatically carry on without asking you. PancakeNotch shows approval buttons only when Codex confirms that a person should answer. If that information isn't available, the request stays in Codex.

There is no "always allow": every request asks you again. If PancakeNotch isn't running, the tool simply asks as usual, so nothing gets stuck.

If something's missing

  • The list is emptyThe tool has to be running. Start a thread in Claude Code, Codex or Cursor, and give it a moment to show up.
  • Codex never says "Needs you"Approval requests from Codex show up only after you've connected it. Check that you typed /hooks in Codex and trusted the PancakeNotch hook.
  • Cursor shows nothingCursor shows up only after you've connected it, and only for the agent in the Cursor app (the IDE).
  • No Allow and Deny buttonsThey appear for Claude Code and Codex after you've connected them. Cursor can't have them.

AI Usage: limits, tokens and accounts #

The AI Usage module shows how much of your Claude and Codex limits are left, and what you've used, for each of your accounts. Turn it on under Modules, the same way as Agents. It's offered once PancakeNotch finds Claude Code or Codex on your Mac.

Limits

Each limit is a bar showing how much is left, with the time until it resets, for example Session 63% left · resets in 2h 14m or Weekly 77% left. A small pace mark on the bar shows where it would be if you used your limit evenly until it resets. When an account drops under 10% left, its gauge shows beside the closed notch, so you notice before you run out.

In AI Usage's Settings you can choose when that gauge shows (Never, When close or Always), which account it follows (or whichever is closest to its limit), and which limit: Session, Weekly or the Closest of the two. The pop-up under the notch follows the same account and limit, and can be switched off.

The bars change color as a limit runs low: green with half or more left, yellow from 20 to 49%, orange from 10 to 19%, and red under 10%.

Codex limits refresh when you open AI Usage (PancakeNotch asks Codex, which checks with OpenAI). Claude limits update when Claude Code next sends a request; older readings show an "as of" time.

Tokens, "API value" and "Cost"

Switch between Today, 7 days and 30 days to see how many tokens you used and which models you used most.

  • API value is for accounts on a plan: what those tokens would cost if you paid for each use. You don't pay it. It shows how much your plan gives you.
  • Cost is for accounts that use an API key: what the tokens really cost.

More than one account

Many people have two or more Claude or Codex accounts. PancakeNotch finds them by itself in ~/.claude, ~/.codex and folders named like ~/.claude-work. If one is missing, click Add account folder… in the AI Usage settings and choose its folder. You can rename an account there too.

The pill at the top right shows the account you're looking at: the tool's icon, the account's name and a small ▾. Click it for a list of your accounts, grouped by tool, each with a tiny bar for how much is left. Pick one to see it, or pick All accounts for the totals.

Emails are partly hidden, with the part before the @ blurred except its first letter, until you click one to show it (click again to hide it, and closing the account list hides it too).

If something's missing

  • No Claude limits yetThey appear after you've used Claude Code once.
  • Codex tokens look lowOlder Codex sessions that Codex has compressed (shrunk to save space) aren't counted.

Token history and Claude limits come from files the tools keep on your Mac. For fresh Codex limits, PancakeNotch asks your installed Codex, which checks with OpenAI using its own sign-in. PancakeNotch never reads sign-in tokens or the Keychain. It saves token counts and model names for 90 days, never conversation text.

Updates #

PancakeNotch checks for a new version once a day, and always asks you before installing anything. When an update is ready, you'll see a small dot on the menu bar icon and a note on Home. Click it to see what's new and choose whether to install.

  • Check now: click the menu bar icon and choose Check for Updates…, or click Check Now under General › Updates.
  • Turn off automatic checks: switch off Check for updates automatically under General › Updates. The welcome offers the same choice.
  • Built it yourself? Copies built from the source code don't update themselves. Download a released version to get updates.

The check only asks GitHub whether there's a newer version. Nothing about you or your Mac is sent.

Launch at login #

To have the notch ready whenever you log in, open the PancakeNotch window, choose General and switch on Launch at login. The welcome offers it too.

If it says Waiting for your approval, macOS wants you to allow it: click Open Login Items and switch on PancakeNotch in System Settings → General → Login Items & Extensions.

Privacy #

  • Everything stays on your Mac. No accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking.
  • One network request: the once-a-day update check, which only asks GitHub for the latest version. You can turn it off (see Updates).
  • Statistics are a file on your Mac. Listening and notch use are counted locally and kept for 90 days. Delete them any time with Clear Statistics on the Statistics page.
  • Agents and AI Usage keep their history on your Mac. Codex checks with OpenAI for fresh limits when AI Usage opens. PancakeNotch reads no sign-in tokens or Keychain, and saves only token counts and model names for 90 days, never conversation text (see AI Usage).
  • Permissions only when needed: controlling Music and Spotify in limited mode, System Audio Recording for the optional "Follow the music" bars, and Login Items if you turn on launch at login.
  • This website has no cookies and no analytics either.

Uninstalling #

  1. If you turned on Launch at login, switch it off under General.
  2. If you connected Claude Code, Codex or Cursor, click Disconnect for each one under Modules › Agents settings. That removes PancakeNotch's helper from the tool's settings.
  3. Quit PancakeNotch: click the menu bar icon and choose Quit PancakeNotch (or use Quit PancakeNotch at the bottom of the window's sidebar).
  4. Drag PancakeNotch from Applications to the Trash.

Optional: remove its data too

In Finder, choose Go → Go to Folder…, paste each location and move what you find to the Trash:

  • ~/Library/Application Support/PancakeNotch holds your statistics and the Shelf.
  • ~/Library/Preferences/io.github.sickpancake.PancakeNotch.plist holds your settings.
  • ~/Library/Caches/io.github.sickpancake.PancakeNotch may hold downloaded updates, if it exists.

Permissions you gave it can be removed in System Settings → Privacy & Security.

Report a problem #

PancakeNotch has no crash reporter and sends nothing automatically, so we only hear about problems you tell us about. Thank you for taking the time.

  1. Open the project's issues on GitHub (you need a free GitHub account).
  2. Say what happened, what you expected, and the steps to make it happen again.
  3. Add these details: your PancakeNotch version (on the About page), your macOS version and your Mac model (both under Apple menu → About This Mac).

The Report a Problem button on the app's Help page opens a report with those details already filled in. Nothing is sent until you submit it. Please leave personal information out of screenshots.
